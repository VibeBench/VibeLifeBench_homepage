Abstract

Large language model agents are increasingly deployed as personal assistants, yet almost all existing evaluations remain confined to short, self-contained requests in static environments and fail to reflect real-world life assistance. Life assistance differs in kind: it requires the agent to be proactive (deciding on its own, without being prompted, when to act, when to contact the user, and when to stay silent, and eliciting the implicit constraints a request only hints at); to operate in a dynamic, living world whose state evolves on its own, where weather, prices, inventory, and itineraries may change silently and only an attentive agent notices and propagates the change; and to be long-horizon, since each task spans a multi-week lifecycle throughout which an evolving plan must stay self-consistent while the constraints given at the outset are always upheld. VibeLifeBench is a benchmark of 200 multi-week tasks across ten everyday-life domains, built on 22 mock service backends that expose 288 tool interfaces and driven by scripted timelines in which many world changes occur silently, with no signal to the agent. Evaluating contemporary strong models shows that they remain far from this goal, exposing a large gap between today's passive, single-turn tool use and long-horizon assistance in a living world.

Three properties current benchmarks overlook

🧭

Proactive

The world changes while no one is prompting the agent. It must re-inspect the world on its own each turn and decide whether to act, to notify the user, or to stay silent, and surface implicit constraints from a request that only hints at them.

🌍

Living world

A stateful world advances on its own clock. A large fraction of changes are silent: the world changes with no notification, so only an agent that re-checks discovers the discrepancy in time and carries it into its plan.

🗓️

Long-horizon

Each task is a scripted multi-week timeline (median 29 days) of preparation, execution, and wrap-up. The agent must keep an evolving plan self-consistent and hold the initial hard constraints throughout.

Headline result

Even the strongest of five contemporary frontier models reaches an avg@3 of only 0.352, with a best-of-3 ceiling of 0.504. Models fail to persistently maintain cross-stage, auditable state, miss silent changes in the living world, decay in coherence over the horizon, and none is competent across all ten life domains.
